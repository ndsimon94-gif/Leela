Experiences

Seven kinds of time.

Nearly everything at Leela belongs to one or more of these seven. They shape the calendar. They also describe an ordinary afternoon, when nothing is scheduled.

The seven

Presence

A place to stop consuming and start noticing

Morning practice is held five days a week and is offered freely. There are dharma talks, silent retreats, walking meditation, and tea prepared with attention.

Meditation · Dharma · Silence · Contemplation · Breath · Tea · Walking · Attention

Wildness

Remembering that humans are animals too

Tracking, fire, water, shelter, and the names of the plants underfoot. Guided time in the backcountry, and prepared, supported time alone on the land.

Foraging · Wilderness skills · Backpacking · Nature awareness · Primitive skills · Solo time · Fasting · Ecology

Play

Adults need recess too

Not everything meaningful needs to be productive. Games, dance, improvisation, exploration, and open time without an agenda.

Games · Dance · Improvisation · Exploration · Costumes · Unstructured time

Making

The intelligence of the hands

Painting, clay, wood, words, food, and music. Process matters more than performance, and no experience is required.

Painting · Drawing · Ceramics · Woodworking · Natural crafts · Writing · Cooking · Gardening · Music

Connection

Where strangers can become neighbors

The weekly community dinner, storytelling evenings, song, and seasonal feasts. Time with elders, and conversation that is allowed to go slowly.

Shared meals · Storytelling · Conversation · Elders · Community work · Fires · Music · Celebration

Restoration

Sometimes the most useful thing is nothing

Gentle yoga, bodywork, long sleep, slow mornings, and days without screens. Rest here is not a reward for productivity.

Yoga · Bodywork · Sauna and cold water (in time) · Hammocks · Sleep · Digital sabbaths · Slow mornings

Stewardship

Leave Leela a little better than you found it

Guests do not only consume Leela. From time to time, they also help care for it. Shared work is one of the most natural ways to arrive in a place.

Gardening · Cooking · Trail work · Stacking wood · Ecological restoration · Building · Caring for shared spaces

What Leela protects

Experiences worth making room for.

Some of the oldest human experiences are being quietly crowded out. Leela sets aside space for them deliberately.

Intergenerational life

Meaningful time between younger and older people. Elders teach craft, practical skills, cooking, music, knowledge of the land, and what they have learned about living. Wisdom does not belong only to professional teachers.

Rites of passage

Considered ways to mark the major transitions of a life, with no religious affiliation required.

Coming of age · Marriage · Parenthood · Divorce · Grief · Retirement · Milestone birthdays

Grief and mortality

Gentle, unhurried spaces for subjects modern culture tends to avoid. Grief walks, remembrance fires, death cafés, memorial gatherings, and conversations about aging.

Boredom

Boredom is not always a problem to solve. Some of Leela is set aside for nothing in particular: no programming, no screens, no instructions. Only time.

Beginner's mind

Adults need places to do things without being good at them. To draw, sing, carve, or write poorly, and to enjoy it.

Shared work

Work can be a practice. Cooking together, stacking wood, tending gardens, repairing trails, washing dishes. Participation is part of being here.

Oral culture

Knowledge passed from one person to another, aloud.

Storytelling · Poetry · Folk song · Myth · Local history · Personal stories

Celebration

Leela is not a place devoted to fixing people. It is also a place to celebrate being alive: feasts, music, dancing, solstice gatherings, harvests, and unplanned joy.
