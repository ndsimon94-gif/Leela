Stay

A few days at the pace of the land.

Stay in a yurt or at the camp. Come for a retreat, a residency, or simply to slow down. It often takes a day or two to fully arrive.

The communal heart

The House

The House holds the kitchen, the dining table, the library, the practice room, and a large fireplace. It is where tea is made, meals are shared, and conversations continue into the evening.

The House is not lodging. It serves as the common room for all guests, and for visitors during land hours.

Within
Kitchen · Dining · Library · Practice room · Fireplace
Open
To guests throughout the day, to visitors during land hours

Accommodation

The Yurts

Simple, beautiful rooms set within the landscape. Each has a proper bed with good linens, warm lamps, a small writing table, and a window toward the hills. Comfortable, and uncluttered.

Washrooms and showers are a short walk away.

Camping

The Camp

Thoughtfully placed campsites with level tent pads, a shared fire circle, and space between neighbors. Campers share the House, meals, and morning practice with all other guests.

Ways to stay

Reasons to spend the night.

Personal retreat

No program is required. Join morning practice, walk the land, read in the library, share meals, and rest.

Retreats

Silent retreats, digital sabbaths, wilderness courses, and multi-day workshops. Lodging and meals are included.

Upcoming retreats

Residencies

Artists, writers, and makers may stay two to six weeks, sharing their work with the community through teaching or open studios. Some residencies are fully funded.

Applications open twice yearly.

Work-trade

Contribute time in the kitchen, garden, or on the trails in exchange for lodging and meals.

About work-trade

In time

Built slowly, and with care.

Leela is currently a house, a small number of yurts, and a camp. This is deliberate. We would rather build carefully, as the land and resources allow, than build quickly.
